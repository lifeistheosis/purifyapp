Amos was no professional prophet but a herdsman and a gatherer of sycamore fruit from Tekoa in Judah, sent in the eighth century before Christ to preach in the prosperous northern kingdom of Israel. He condemns the nations around Israel and then Israel itself, for selling the poor, crushing the needy, and trusting in feasts and sacrifices while justice is trampled. He warns that the day of the Lord will be darkness and not light, and sees visions of the judgment to come. The book ends with God's promise to raise up again the fallen tabernacle of David.

At the council of the apostles in Jerusalem, Saint James cited that promise in its Greek form to show that the Gentiles too were called to seek the Lord. Saint Irenaeus heard in Amos's sun going down at noon a foretelling of the darkness at the Crucifixion.
