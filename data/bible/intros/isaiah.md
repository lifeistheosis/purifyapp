Isaiah son of Amoz prophesied in eighth-century Jerusalem and is called by the Church the "Old Testament Evangelist" for the clarity with which he foretold Christ.

The Church reads Isaiah's prophecies at almost every major feast: "Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son" (7:14) at the Nativity; "A child is born to us" (9:6) at the Christmas Royal Hours; the Suffering Servant of chapter 53 throughout Holy Week. The vision of the seraphim crying "Holy, Holy, Holy" (6:3) is the source of the Trisagion sung at every Liturgy.

Isaiah is read at Vespers throughout Great Lent. Brenton's Septuagint preserves some readings, most famously 7:14, that differ from the Hebrew Masoretic text and which the Apostles cite directly.
