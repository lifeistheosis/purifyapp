First Maccabees is the history of the Jewish struggle for faith and freedom in the second century before Christ. When the Syrian Greek king Antiochus Epiphanes forbids the law of Moses and sets up an idol on the altar of the Temple, the aged priest Mattathias refuses to offer the pagan sacrifice and flees to the hills with his five sons. His son Judas, called Maccabeus, leads the revolt, wins back Jerusalem, and cleanses and rededicates the Temple. After Judas falls in battle his brothers Jonathan and Simon carry on the struggle, and the book ends with the death of Simon and the rule of his son John.

The eight days of rededication, first appointed in this book, became the feast of the Dedication, and Saint John's Gospel shows Christ in the Temple at that feast, walking in Solomon's porch. The Orthodox Church receives the book with the Septuagint as a witness of costly faithfulness to God in a time of persecution.
