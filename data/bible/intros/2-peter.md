The Second Epistle of Peter is the apostle's farewell, written as he knew his death was near, as the Lord had shown him. He calls the faithful to grow from faith through virtue, knowledge, self-control, patience, godliness and brotherly kindness into love, and teaches that through God's great promises we may become sharers in the divine nature. He recalls standing with Christ on the holy mountain at the Transfiguration and hearing the Father's voice, warns against false teachers and against scoffers who mock the delay of the Lord's coming, and answers that God does not count time as we do: He is patient, giving room for repentance, and the day will surely come.

Its words on sharing in the divine nature are a cornerstone of the Orthodox teaching on theosis, and its witness to the Transfiguration is the Epistle at the Liturgy of that feast. Its place in the canon was settled later than that of most books, but the Church receives it as Peter's.
