The Epistle of Jeremiah is a single chapter, presented as a letter the prophet sent to the captives about to be led away to Babylon. It warns them that there they will see gods of silver, gold and wood carried on men's shoulders, which fill the nations with fear, and it mocks such idols at length: they cannot speak or see, cannot save themselves from rust, fire or thieves, must have the dust wiped from their faces, and are no gods at all. Again and again comes the refrain that the people should not fear them.

The King James Bible prints this letter as the sixth chapter of Baruch, while the Septuagint keeps it as its own book after Lamentations. Saint Athanasius and Saint Cyril of Jerusalem counted it with Jeremiah, Baruch and Lamentations as a single book. The Church reads it as a call to worship the living God alone.
