The Acts of the Apostles, written by Saint Luke as the sequel to his Gospel, recounts the descent of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, the birth of the Church in Jerusalem, and the missionary journeys that carried the gospel from Judea through Asia Minor to Rome.

For the Orthodox Church, Acts is the book of Pentecost season: it is read in full from Pascha through Pentecost, one chapter a day at Liturgy. Its scenes of common life, breaking bread house to house, and the laying on of hands in the Spirit are the pattern of all later Christian community.

Acts also documents the first Council of Jerusalem (chapter 15), the conciliar mind of the Church that would shape every later Ecumenical Council.
