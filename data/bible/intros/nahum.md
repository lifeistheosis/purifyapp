Nahum of Elkosh foretold the fall of Nineveh, the capital of Assyria, the empire that had destroyed the northern kingdom of Israel and terrorized the nations for generations. His short book opens with a hymn to the Lord, slow to anger yet great in power, a stronghold for those who trust Him and a terror to His enemies, and then paints in vivid, rushing poetry the siege, the chariots racing in the streets, and the sack of the city. Nineveh fell in 612 BC.

Read beside Jonah, Nahum shows both sides of God's dealings with the nations: the city that repented at Jonah's preaching was spared, but when it returned to its cruelty, judgment came. Amid the doom stands the picture of a messenger on the mountains bringing good tidings of peace, which Christians have heard as a figure of the preaching of the Gospel.
