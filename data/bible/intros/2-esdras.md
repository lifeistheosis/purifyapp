The book printed as 2 Esdras in the King James Apocrypha is the one the Slavonic Bible calls the Third Book of Esdras. Its heart, chapters 3 to 14, is a Jewish apocalypse written after the Romans destroyed Jerusalem in AD 70 and set in the mouth of Ezra in Babylon. In a series of visions Ezra wrestles with the angel Uriel, whom the Church counts among the archangels, over the hardest questions: why God let His people fall to a more wicked nation, why so few are saved, and when the end will come. He is shown the signs of the end, the Messiah, and the judgment, and at last restores the lost Scriptures to his people. The two opening and two closing chapters are later Christian additions.

The book survives in Latin and other ancient versions but not in Greek, and it is not part of the Septuagint. The Slavonic Bible keeps it, but the Church does not read it in her services.
