Second Kingdoms, called 2 Samuel in most English Bibles, is the story of David's reign. After the death of Saul, David is made king, first over Judah and then over all Israel. He takes Jerusalem, brings the ark of the covenant into the city with dancing, and receives through the prophet Nathan God's promise that his house and his throne will stand forever. Then comes his fall: his adultery with Bathsheba and the death of her husband Uriah, Nathan's rebuke, David's repentance, and the sorrows that follow in his own house, above all the revolt and death of his son Absalom.

The Church hears Nathan's promise fulfilled in Christ, the Son of David, whose kingdom, as the Creed confesses, is without end. Tradition sets Psalm 50, the great psalm of repentance prayed again and again in the daily services, in the hour when Nathan came to David.
