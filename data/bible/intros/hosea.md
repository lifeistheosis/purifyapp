Hosea prophesied in the northern kingdom of Israel in the eighth century before Christ, in its last prosperous and faithless years. God commands him to marry an unfaithful wife, and his own heartbreak becomes the image of the Lord's love for a people who run after other gods: God will punish them, yet He cannot give them up, and He will win His bride back and heal her faithlessness. In the Greek Bible the twelve minor prophets form a single book, and Hosea stands first.

Christ twice recalled Hosea's word that God desires mercy and not sacrifice. The Church hears in his promise of being raised up on the third day a foretelling of the Resurrection, and his taunt against death and Hades, taken up by Saint Paul, rings out every Pascha in the homily of Saint John Chrysostom. In this translation he is called Osee.
