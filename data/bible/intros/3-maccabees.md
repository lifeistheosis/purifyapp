Third Maccabees has nothing to do with Judas and his brothers; it shares their name because it tells of another persecution faithfully endured. It is set in the reign of the Egyptian king Ptolemy Philopator, half a century before the Maccabean revolt. After his victory at Raphia, the king tries to force his way into the Holy of Holies at Jerusalem and is struck down at the prayer of the high priest Simon. Back in Egypt he takes revenge on the Jews, herds them into the hippodrome at Alexandria, and orders them trampled by drunken elephants. When the aged priest Eleazar prays, two angels appear, the elephants turn on the king's own troops, and the king repents and sets the Jews free.

The Greek Church receives this book with the Septuagint, and the Apostolic Canons count three books of Maccabees; it also stands in the Slavonic Bible. It is a witness to God's care for His people scattered among the nations.
