Habakkuk, prophesying as the Babylonian power rose in the late seventh century before Christ, dares to question God: how long will violence go unpunished, and why does He punish Judah by a crueler nation? He takes his stand on his watch to wait for an answer, and receives it: the vision will come in its time, and the just will live by faith. The book ends with his prayer, a song of trust that rejoices in God even when the fig tree bears no fruit.

Saint Paul built on that answer in Romans and Galatians. Habakkuk's prayer is the fourth of the biblical odes behind the canon at Matins, and the Church, reading the Greek, hears in it Christ made known between two living creatures and the Theotokos as the shaded mountain from which the Holy One comes. In the Paschal canon of Saint John of Damascus, Habakkuk keeps his watch beside us to see the angel announce the Resurrection.
