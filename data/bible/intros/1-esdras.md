The First Book of Esdras is a Greek retelling of the return from exile. It begins with King Josiah's great Passover and the fall of Jerusalem, then follows the decree of Cyrus, the return of the exiles, the rebuilding of the Temple and the reforms of Ezra, closely parallel to the end of Chronicles, to Ezra, and to part of Nehemiah. At its center stands a story found nowhere else: three young guardsmen of King Darius contend over what is strongest. One says wine, another the king, and the third, Zerubbabel, women, and above all truth. He wins, and asks as his reward that Jerusalem and the Temple be rebuilt.

The Greek Bible counts this book first among the books of Esdras, while the Slavonic Bible calls it the Second Book of Esdras; it is not the book printed here as 2 Esdras. Saint Augustine heard in its praise of truth a word about Christ, who is the Truth.
