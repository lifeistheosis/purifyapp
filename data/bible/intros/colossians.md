Saint Paul wrote to the Colossians from prison, to a church in Phrygia that he had never visited, founded by his fellow worker Epaphras. Teachers there were mixing the gospel with philosophy, the worship of angels, and rules about food and holy days. Paul answers with one of the great hymns of the New Testament: Christ is the visible image of the unseen God, firstborn over all creation, in whom all things were made and hold together, and the head of His body, the Church; in Him the fullness of God dwells in a body. Those who have died and risen with Him in Baptism are to set their minds on things above and put on the new man.

The letter was carried by Tychicus together with Onesimus, the subject of the letter to Philemon, and Paul asked that it be read at Laodicea as well. It mentions Luke, the beloved physician. Saint John Chrysostom preached twelve homilies on it.
