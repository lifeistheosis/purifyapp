Zechariah began to prophesy in 520 BC beside Haggai, encouraging the returned exiles to rebuild the Temple. His book opens with eight visions in the night: horsemen sent to patrol the earth, a man with a measuring line, the high priest Joshua stripped of his filthy garments and clothed anew, a golden lampstand between two olive trees, and more, all promising that God will dwell again in Jerusalem. The later chapters look further ahead: a king coming to Zion, just and lowly and riding on a donkey; a shepherd valued at thirty pieces of silver; and a day when the Lord's feet will stand on the Mount of Olives.

The Church reads the coming of the lowly King at Vespers of Palm Sunday, the thirty pieces of silver at the Royal Hours of Holy Friday, and the Mount of Olives at Vespers of the Ascension.
