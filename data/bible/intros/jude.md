The Epistle of Jude was written by Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James, whom the Church honors as Jude the brother of the Lord. He urges the faithful to fight for the faith handed down to the saints once for all, because ungodly men had slipped in among them, turning God's grace into license and denying the Lord. He recalls God's judgments on unbelieving Israel in the wilderness, on the angels who left their proper dwelling, and on Sodom and Gomorrah, and calls the intruders waterless clouds and wandering stars. He also recalls Michael's dispute over the body of Moses and the prophecy of Enoch.

Jude closes with one of the most beautiful doxologies in Scripture, to the God who can keep the faithful from stumbling and bring them blameless and rejoicing before His glory. The Church reads the letter at the Liturgy in the last days before Great Lent.
