Nehemiah, cupbearer to the Persian king Artaxerxes, hears that the walls of Jerusalem still lie in ruins. He weeps, fasts and prays, wins the king's leave, and goes up as governor to rebuild them. Against mockery, threats and plots, the people build with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other, and the wall is finished in fifty-two days. Then Ezra reads the law aloud to the gathered people from early morning until midday; they weep, keep the feast of Tabernacles with joy, confess their sins, and bind themselves by covenant to keep the law. Much of the book is told in Nehemiah's own words.

Christian readers have seen in Nehemiah a pattern of prayer joined to labor, and in the great reading of the law a foreshadowing of the Scriptures read aloud at the heart of the Church's worship. Second Maccabees remembers him as one who built the Temple and the altar and recovered the sacred fire.
