Malachi, whose name means my messenger, is the last of the twelve minor prophets. Speaking after the Temple had been rebuilt, he rebukes a weary people and their priests for offering God blind and lame animals, for breaking faith with the wives of their youth, and for robbing Him of the tithes. He declares that from the rising of the sun to its setting God's name is great among the nations, where a pure offering is made to Him, and he promises that the Lord will send His messenger to prepare the way before Him, and Elijah before the great day of the Lord.

The Gospels apply that messenger to Saint John the Forerunner. The Didache and Saint Justin Martyr saw the pure offering fulfilled in the Eucharist offered in every place, and the troparion of the Nativity hails Christ by Malachi's name for Him, the Sun of Righteousness.
