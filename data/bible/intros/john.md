The Gospel according to John, written by the apostle and evangelist John the Theologian, is the most contemplative and Christological of the four. Its prologue - "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" - is read at the Paschal Liturgy in every Orthodox parish, in many languages at once, proclaiming the divinity of Christ to the world.

John focuses on seven "signs" of Christ and seven "I am" sayings, drawing the reader steadily into the mystery of the Son's relationship with the Father. His discourses on the Bread of Life (chapter 6), the Good Shepherd (chapter 10), and the High Priestly Prayer (chapter 17) are foundational to Eucharistic and Trinitarian theology.

The Church reads John continuously from Pascha through Pentecost - the season of the risen Christ. Saint John Chrysostom and Saint Cyril of Alexandria left major commentaries on this Gospel.
