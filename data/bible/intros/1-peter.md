The First Epistle of Peter was written by Saint Peter from Rome, which he calls Babylon, with the help of Silvanus, to Christians scattered across Asia Minor under persecution. He reminds them that they have been born anew to a living hope through the resurrection of Christ, ransomed by His precious blood like that of a spotless lamb, and built as living stones into a spiritual house, a royal priesthood and a holy nation. He teaches them to bear unjust suffering as Christ did, to live honorably among unbelievers, and to entrust all their anxieties to God, who cares for them.

The letter is one of the chief witnesses in Scripture to Christ's descent into Hades, where He preached to the spirits in prison, the mystery shown in the Orthodox icon of the Resurrection. It also sees in Noah's ark a figure of Baptism. Saint Polycarp drew on it again and again in his own letter to the Philippians.
