Lamentations is five poems of grief over Jerusalem after the Babylonians destroyed it in 586 BC. The city sits alone like a widow, her Temple burned, her children starving or led away captive. Four of the poems are acrostics, their verses following the order of the Hebrew alphabet, and this translation keeps the names of the letters in the text. The Greek opens with a line telling how Jeremiah sat weeping and made this lament over Jerusalem, and the Church receives it as his.

Through the grief runs a confession that the disaster was deserved, and a stubborn hope in the mercies of God that never come to an end. Early Fathers, Saint Justin Martyr among them, read as a word about Christ the verse that, in the Greek, calls the anointed Lord the breath of our nostrils, taken in the snares of the enemy.
