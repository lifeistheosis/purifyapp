Esther tells how a Jewish orphan became queen of Persia and saved her people. Brought up by her cousin Mordecai, she is chosen as queen by King Artaxerxes, as the Greek text names him. When the king's chief minister Haman, enraged that Mordecai will not bow to him, obtains a decree to destroy every Jew in the empire, Esther risks her life by going before the king unbidden, and the plot falls back on Haman himself. The feast of Purim keeps the memory of that deliverance.

The Greek text read by the Church carries six additions not found in the Hebrew, among them Mordecai's dream and the prayers of Mordecai and Esther, so that God, never named in the Hebrew book, is openly called upon. In this translation most of them stand in square brackets, Mordecai is Mardochaeus and Haman is Aman. Saint Clement of Rome praised Esther for risking her life to save the twelve tribes.
