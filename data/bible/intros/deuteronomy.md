Deuteronomy, the fifth book of Moses, takes its Greek name from the second giving of the law. On the plains of Moab, Moses speaks to a new generation: he recalls the journey from Sinai, repeats the Ten Commandments, calls Israel to love the one Lord with all their heart, soul and strength, and sets before them life and death, blessing and curse. The book closes with his song, his blessing of the tribes, and his death on the mountain looking over the land he would not enter.

Christ answered each of the devil's temptations in the wilderness from Deuteronomy, and named its command to love God the first and greatest. Saint Peter and Saint Stephen preached Christ as the prophet like Moses whom this book foretold.

The Song of Moses is the second of the biblical odes behind the canon at Matins, and passages from Deuteronomy are read at Vespers on the Sunday of the Fathers of the First Ecumenical Council.
