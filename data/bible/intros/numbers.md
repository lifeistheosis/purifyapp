Numbers, the fourth book of Moses, takes its name from the two counts of the people it records. It follows Israel from Sinai through forty years in the wilderness: the priestly blessing, the spies and the people's refusal to enter the land, the rebellion of Korah, the water from the rock, the bronze serpent, and the prophet Balaam, hired to curse Israel, who blessed it instead.

Christ pointed to the serpent lifted up in the wilderness as a sign of His own lifting up on the Cross, and Saint Paul saw Christ in the rock that gave water. Balaam's prophecy of a star rising out of Jacob is read at Vespers of the Nativity, and the Church's hymns see the Theotokos in the dry rod of Aaron that blossomed.

At Vespers of Pentecost the Church reads how the Spirit came upon the seventy elders, and the wish of Moses that all the Lord's people were prophets.
