Paul's Epistle to the Romans is the longest and most systematic of his letters, written near the end of his third missionary journey to a Church he had not yet visited. It is the apostle's most sustained exposition of the gospel: the universal need of salvation, justification by faith, life in the Spirit, and the place of Israel and the nations in God's plan.

For the Orthodox Church, Romans 6 - baptism into Christ's death and resurrection - is the epistle read at every baptismal Liturgy. Chapters 8 and 12 shape Orthodox teaching on the indwelling Spirit and the spiritual life as a "living sacrifice."

Saint John Chrysostom called Romans "the most profound of all Scripture" and wrote 32 homilies on it.
