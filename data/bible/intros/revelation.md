The Revelation (Greek: Ἀποκάλυψις, "unveiling") was given to the apostle and evangelist John on the island of Patmos and is the only prophetic book of the New Testament. Through vivid visions of the throne of God, the Lamb who was slain, the seven seals, and the new Jerusalem, it unveils the meaning of the Church's struggle in history and the certainty of Christ's victory.

Uniquely among the books of the New Testament, Revelation is **not read aloud in Orthodox liturgical services**. The Church has preserved it as Scripture and studied it deeply - Saint Andrew of Caesarea wrote the standard Orthodox commentary in the sixth century - but reserves its public reading out of pastoral care, knowing how easily its imagery is misused.

Read alongside the Fathers, the book reveals the Liturgy: the heavenly worship of the Lamb, the saints with their bowls of incense, the marriage supper. Every Eucharist is a participation in what John saw.
