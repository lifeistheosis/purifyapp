Saint Paul wrote to Titus, a Greek convert and trusted companion, whom he had left in Crete to set the churches in order and appoint elders in every town. The short letter describes the man a bishop must be, blameless, hospitable, self-controlled and holding fast to sound teaching, and warns against idle talkers and deceivers. Paul asks every age and station to adorn the teaching of God by their lives.

Twice the letter sums up the whole gospel: the grace of God has appeared to all people, teaching us to live soberly as we await the appearing of Christ; and God saved us not for our works but by His mercy, through the washing of rebirth and the renewal of the Holy Spirit. These passages, with their language of God appearing and of washing, are the Epistle read at the Liturgy of Theophany. The Church honors Titus as the first bishop of Crete, and Saint John Chrysostom preached six homilies on the letter.
