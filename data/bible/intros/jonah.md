Jonah is the prophet who ran from God. Sent to preach to Nineveh, the great city of Israel's enemies, he takes ship in the opposite direction, is thrown into the sea in a storm, and is swallowed by a great fish, in whose belly he spends three days and three nights and prays before he is cast up on dry land. Sent again, he preaches, and the whole city, from the king down to the cattle, repents in sackcloth, and God spares it. The book ends with Jonah angry at God's mercy, and with God's gentle question whether He should not pity so great a city.

Christ named the sign of Jonah as the sign of His own three days in the heart of the earth, and the Church reads the whole book at the Vesperal Liturgy of Holy Saturday. Jonah's prayer from the belly of the fish is the sixth of the biblical odes behind the canon at Matins.
