The Second Epistle to the Thessalonians followed soon after the first, because some in the church had been shaken by a claim that the day of the Lord was already at hand, and some had stopped working. Saint Paul answers that the day will not come until there is first a falling away and the man of sin is revealed, the son of perdition, who will set himself up in the temple of God until the Lord destroys him at His coming; for now something holds him back. Meanwhile the faithful are to stand firm, keep working quietly, and never tire of doing good.

His charge to hold fast the traditions they were taught, whether by word or by letter, is one of the clearest witnesses in Scripture to Holy Tradition. Saint Cyril of Jerusalem drew on this letter in teaching his catechumens about the Antichrist, and Saint John Chrysostom preached five homilies on it.
