Judith is the story of a people saved by a widow's courage and prayer. Holofernes, Nebuchadnezzar's general, besieges the small town of Bethulia, and its leaders promise to surrender if God does not help them within five days. Judith, a devout widow who lives in fasting and prayer, rebukes them for putting God to the test, prays, and goes out with her maid into the enemy camp. There, after a banquet, she beheads the drunken Holofernes with his own sword, and the leaderless army flees. The book closes with her song of praise and the peace of her long life.

The Church receives Judith with the Septuagint among the books worthy to be read, and Saint Athanasius named it among those appointed for the instruction of new believers. Saint Clement of Rome, near the end of the first century, held her up as a woman who risked her life for her people. Her victory shows God saving through the weak.
