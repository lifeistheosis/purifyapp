Daniel was carried to Babylon as a youth with his companions Ananias, Azarias and Misael, and all four kept faith at the court of the king. The first half of the book tells of the king's dream of a statue struck down by a stone cut without hands, the three youths unharmed in the furnace with a fourth like the Son of God, and Daniel in the den of lions. His visions include one like a son of man coming with the clouds to the Ancient of Days. The Greek adds the prayer and song of the three youths, Susanna, and Bel and the Dragon.

Christ applied that vision of the Son of Man to Himself before the high priest. The prayer and song of the three youths are the seventh and eighth biblical odes, and the Church reads Daniel at the Nativity and on Holy Saturday. Saint Hippolytus of Rome wrote one of the oldest Christian commentaries on it.
