The Third Epistle of John, the shortest book of the New Testament, is a personal letter from the elder to his beloved friend Gaius. He prays that Gaius may be well in body as he is in soul, and praises him for walking in the truth and for welcoming traveling brothers, strangers to him, who had gone out for the sake of the Name and took nothing from the pagans. In contrast he rebukes Diotrephes, who loves to be first, rejects the elder's authority, refuses to receive the brethren, and puts out of the church those who would. He commends a certain Demetrius, and closes hoping to see Gaius soon.

The Church receives this letter, with the second, as the work of Saint John the Theologian in his old age. Brief as it is, it gives a living glimpse of the early Church: missionaries on the road, households that sheltered them, and the duty of Christian hospitality.
