Ezekiel, a priest carried to Babylon with the first exiles, was called in 593 BC by an overwhelming vision of God's glory: four living creatures with the faces of a man, a lion, an ox and an eagle, and above them a throne bearing the likeness of a man. Through strange signs and visions he warned of Jerusalem's fall and saw the Lord's glory leave the Temple. After the fall he spoke of hope: God Himself will shepherd His flock, give His people a new heart and spirit, breathe life into a valley of dry bones, and dwell in a new Temple.

The Church came to see the four living creatures as figures of the four Evangelists. She reads Ezekiel at the Sixth Hour in Holy Week, the dry bones at Matins of Holy Saturday, and, at the feasts of the Theotokos, the eastern gate that stays shut because the Lord has passed through it, a figure of her ever-virginity.
