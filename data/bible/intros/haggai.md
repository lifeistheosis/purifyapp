Haggai spoke in 520 BC, the second year of King Darius, to the exiles who had come home from Babylon and then let the Temple lie in ruins while they built their own fine houses. In four short messages he urges the governor Zerubbabel, the high priest Joshua and all the people to take courage and build, promising that the Lord is with them and that the glory of this latter house will be greater than the former. The people listen, and within weeks the work begins.

Christian readers have seen that promise fulfilled when Christ Himself came into the Temple, and in the Church He builds. The Epistle to the Hebrews takes up Haggai's word that God will once more shake heaven and earth. Zerubbabel, whom God promises to make as a seal, appears in both genealogies of Christ in the Gospels. In this translation the prophet is Aggaeus and the high priest is called Jesus.
