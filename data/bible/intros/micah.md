Micah of Moresheth, a countryman from the lowlands of Judah, prophesied in the eighth century before Christ, in the days of Isaiah. He speaks against rulers, priests and prophets who devour the poor and sell their judgments, and he foretells the fall of both Samaria and Jerusalem. Yet he also sees the mountain of the Lord's house raised up, the nations streaming to it, and swords beaten into ploughshares, in words almost the same as Isaiah's. He sums up what the Lord asks of man: justice, mercy, and readiness to walk with God.

Above all, Micah named Bethlehem as the town from which a ruler of Israel would come forth, one whose origin is from eternity. The chief priests and scribes gave this answer when Herod asked where the Christ was to be born, and the Church reads it at the services of the Nativity.
