Jeremiah, of a priestly family of Anathoth, was called as a youth to be a prophet to the nations and preached in Jerusalem for some forty years, from the days of King Josiah until the city fell to Babylon in 586 BC. He warned of judgment, wept over the people's unfaithfulness, and suffered for it: he was beaten, imprisoned, let down into a muddy pit, and at last carried off to Egypt. Yet he also bought a field as a sign of hope and foretold a new covenant written on the heart.

The Church sees in him a figure of Christ, the innocent lamb led to the slaughter, in a passage read at the Royal Hours of Holy Friday, and reads the promise of the new covenant at the Vesperal Liturgy of Holy Saturday. The Greek text is shorter than the Hebrew and orders some chapters differently, so numbers here may differ from other Bibles: the new covenant is in chapter 38.
