The Wisdom of Jesus the son of Sirach, also called Ecclesiasticus, is a great collection of teaching by a sage of Jerusalem, written in Hebrew about two centuries before Christ and translated into Greek in Egypt by his grandson. Its first chapter, like Proverbs, makes the fear of the Lord the beginning of wisdom, and from there it ranges over the whole of life: prayer and repentance, almsgiving and humility, friendship, the guarding of the tongue, family, wealth, and even the honor due to physicians. Wisdom herself speaks, telling how she came to dwell in Israel. Near its end comes a long praise of the famous men of old, from Enoch to the high priest Simon.

Saint Athanasius named it among the books the Fathers appointed to be read by those newly coming to the faith, and the Fathers drew on its counsels again and again in their teaching on the Christian life.
