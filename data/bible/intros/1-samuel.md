In the Septuagint, the books of Samuel and Kings form one history in four parts, called 1 to 4 Kingdoms, and this is the first. It opens with Hannah, a childless woman whose prayer in the sanctuary is answered with a son, Samuel, the last of the judges and the prophet who anointed Israel's first kings. It tells of the ark of the covenant captured and returned, of Saul made king and then rejected for his disobedience, and of the shepherd boy David, anointed among his brothers, who strikes down Goliath and flees from Saul's jealousy until Saul falls in battle.

Hannah's song of thanksgiving is the third of the biblical odes behind the canon at Matins. It ends by exalting the Lord's anointed, and the Theotokos echoes it in her own song in Saint Luke's Gospel. In this translation Hannah is called Anna, and Eli the priest is Heli.
