The Second Epistle of John is a short note from the elder to the elect lady and her children, perhaps a Christian woman and her family, or, as many hold, a local church. The elder rejoices that some of her children walk in the truth and repeats the commandment heard from the beginning, that we love one another. He warns that many deceivers have gone out who do not confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh, and forbids welcoming such teachers into the house. He hopes soon to speak with them face to face, so that their joy may be full.

The Church receives the letter as the work of Saint John the Theologian, who here calls himself simply the elder, and Saint Irenaeus quoted it in the second century as the word of the Lord's disciple. In a few lines it holds together what John's first letter teaches at length: love and truth cannot be separated.
