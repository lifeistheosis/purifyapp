Leviticus, the third book of Moses, takes its name from the Levites, the priestly tribe. It is the rule of Israel's worship: the offerings and sacrifices, the consecration of Aaron and his sons, the laws of clean and unclean, the Day of Atonement, the feasts and the jubilee. Through it all runs one call, to be holy because the Lord who dwells among His people is holy.

The Church reads Leviticus through the Epistle to the Hebrews, which shows its sacrifices, and the high priest entering the holy place once a year, as shadows of Christ, the true High Priest who offered Himself once for all. Christ named its command to love one's neighbor as the second of the two great commandments.

Its law for a mother after childbirth stands behind the feast of the Meeting of the Lord, when the Theotokos brought her Son to the Temple with the offering of the poor, and part of it is read at Vespers of that feast.
