Obadiah is the shortest of the prophetic books, a single chapter of judgment on Edom, the nation descended from Esau, Jacob's brother. When Jerusalem was sacked, Edom stood by and gloated, joined in the looting, and handed over those who fled. The prophet declares that Edom's pride, secure in its dwellings high among the rocks, will be brought low, that it will suffer what it has done to others, and that the day of the Lord is near upon all the nations. The book ends with deliverance on Mount Zion and the promise that the kingdom will belong to the Lord.

Tradition, followed in the Church's lives of the saints, identifies the prophet with Obadiah the steward of King Ahab, who hid a hundred prophets of the Lord from Jezebel. Christian readers have heard in the fall of Edom the fall of every pride raised against God, and in the closing promise the kingdom of Christ.
