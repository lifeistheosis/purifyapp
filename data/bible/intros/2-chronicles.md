Second Paralipomenon, or 2 Chronicles, carries the history from Solomon to the end of the kingdom of Judah. It dwells on the building and dedication of the Temple, when fire came down from heaven and the glory of the Lord filled the house, and then follows the kings of David's line alone, weighing each by his faithfulness to the Lord and His house. The reforms of Hezekiah and Josiah, with their great celebrations of the Passover, shine among the failures. It ends with the burning of Jerusalem and, after the seventy years foretold by Jeremiah, the decree of Cyrus that sent the exiles home to rebuild.

Here too is King Manasseh, among the worst of Judah's kings, who was carried in chains to Babylon, humbled himself, prayed, and was brought back to his kingdom. The Church keeps the Prayer of Manasseh among her texts and reads it at Great Compline, a sign that no sinner is beyond repentance.
