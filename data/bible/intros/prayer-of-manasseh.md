The Prayer of Manasseh gives voice to the repentance of one of the most wicked kings of Judah. Second Chronicles tells how Manasseh filled the house of the Lord with idols, how he was carried away to Babylon in chains, and how in his affliction he humbled himself and prayed, and God heard him and brought him back to his kingdom. This short prayer is received as his words in that hour. It praises the Creator, whose mercy is as boundless as His power, confesses sins more numerous than the sand of the sea, and, bending the knee of the heart, asks for forgiveness.

The Orthodox Church reads this prayer at Great Compline, served on weekdays in Great Lent, so that every penitent may pray with Manasseh. Some Greek manuscripts place it among the odes that follow the Psalter, and the Slavonic Bible sets it at the end of Second Chronicles.
