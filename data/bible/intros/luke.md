Saint Luke, "the beloved physician" (Col. 4:14) and companion of the apostle Paul, wrote both this Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles as a two-volume account dedicated to Theophilus. Of all the evangelists, Luke pays the closest attention to the Theotokos, to the poor, and to women among Christ's followers.

His Gospel preserves the Annunciation and Visitation, the Magnificat, the Nunc Dimittis, and the parables of the Prodigal Son and the Good Samaritan - passages that shape Orthodox hymnography in every season of the year.

Tradition holds that Luke was also an iconographer who painted the first icon of the Mother of God. The Church reads Luke continuously from mid-autumn through the start of Great Lent.
