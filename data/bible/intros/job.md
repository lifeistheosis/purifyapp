Job is the story of a righteous man tested to the limit. With God's permission the devil strips him of his flocks, his children and his health, until he sits on a dunghill outside the city, covered with sores. Three friends come to comfort him and end by accusing him, insisting that such suffering must be the punishment of sin, while Job protests his innocence and cries out for God to answer him. At last the Lord speaks out of the whirlwind, shows him the wonders of creation, and restores him. The Greek text adds at the end that Job will be among those the Lord raises from the dead.

The Church reads Job at Vespers through Holy Week, where the innocent sufferer points to Christ, and honors him among the saints on May 6. Saint Gregory the Great, called in the East the Dialogist, devoted to this book his vast Morals on the Book of Job.
