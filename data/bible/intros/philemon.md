The Epistle to Philemon is the shortest of Saint Paul's letters, written from prison to a Christian of Colossae in whose house the church met. Philemon's slave Onesimus had run away, perhaps after wronging his master, and had found his way to Paul, who brought him to Christ. Paul sends him back with this letter, asking Philemon to receive him no longer as a slave but as a beloved brother, and to charge anything he owes to Paul's own account. Without a word of command, the letter shows the gospel changing the bonds between people from within.

Saint John Chrysostom answered those who thought so small a letter unworthy of a place in Scripture, and preached three homilies on it. The Church honors Philemon and Onesimus among the saints, and tradition holds that Onesimus became a bishop and died a martyr.
