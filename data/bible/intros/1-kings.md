Third Kingdoms, called 1 Kings in most English Bibles, opens with the death of David and the reign of Solomon: his prayer for wisdom, his famous judgments, and the building and dedication of the Temple in Jerusalem, when the glory of the Lord filled the house. After Solomon turns to the gods of his foreign wives, the kingdom splits in two, Israel in the north and Judah in the south. The closing chapters belong to the prophet Elijah, who stands against King Ahab and Queen Jezebel, raises the son of the widow of Sarepta, calls down fire on Mount Carmel, and meets God on Horeb not in wind, earthquake or fire but in a gentle breeze.

Christ recalled Elijah's mission to the widow of Sarepta, a woman outside Israel. The Church reads the raising of her son at the Vesperal Liturgy of Holy Saturday, Elijah at Horeb at Vespers of the Transfiguration, and his story at his own feast on July 20.
