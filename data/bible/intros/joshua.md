The Book of Joshua tells how Joshua, the son of Nun, called Naue in this translation, succeeded Moses and led Israel into the promised land. The people cross the Jordan on dry ground, the walls of Jericho fall, Rahab is spared for hiding the spies, the land is won and divided among the tribes, and at the end Joshua gathers the people to renew the covenant and to choose whom they will serve.

In Greek, Joshua bears the same name as Jesus, and the Fathers saw in him a figure of Christ, who leads His people into the true land of promise. The King James text of Hebrews even calls him Jesus. Rahab is remembered among the ancestors of Christ and held up as an example in the Epistles to the Hebrews and of James.

The crossing of the Jordan is read at Vespers of Theophany, and Joshua's meeting with the captain of the Lord's host at the Vesperal Liturgy of Holy Saturday.
