Saint Paul's Second Epistle to the Corinthians is his most personal letter, written from Macedonia about AD 56 after a painful visit and a letter written in tears. Rival teachers had turned some in Corinth against him, and Paul defends his apostleship by pointing not to his strength but to his weakness and sufferings for Christ. He also gives some of his deepest teaching: the glory of the new covenant, the treasure carried in earthen vessels, the new creation in Christ, and the ministry of reconciliation. He appeals for the collection for the poor in Jerusalem, and tells of being caught up into paradise and of the thorn in the flesh that taught him grace is enough.

The Church reads the letter at the Liturgy in its turn after Pentecost, and its closing Trinitarian blessing is the blessing with which the priest opens the Anaphora. Saint John Chrysostom preached thirty homilies on it.
