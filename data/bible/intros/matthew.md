The Gospel according to Matthew is the first of the four Gospels and the bridge from the Old Testament to the New. Written by the apostle Matthew, a former tax collector, it presents Jesus Christ as the long-awaited Messiah, the Son of David, and the new Moses giving a new Law from the mountain.

The Church reads Matthew continuously from Pentecost through mid-autumn - the longest stretch of any Gospel in the lectionary year. Its Sermon on the Mount (chapters 5–7), its account of the Nativity and the Magi (chapter 2), and its commission to baptize "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit" (28:19) are foundational to Orthodox worship and doctrine.

Saint John Chrysostom's 90 homilies on Matthew are among the most beloved patristic commentaries on any biblical book.
