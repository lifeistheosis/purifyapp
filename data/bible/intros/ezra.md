Ezra tells of the return from Babylon. In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia the exiles are allowed to go home; under Zerubbabel and the high priest they rebuild the altar, and through long opposition, stirred on by the prophets Haggai and Zechariah, they finish the Temple. Decades later Ezra himself, a priest and a scribe skilled in the law of Moses, comes up from Babylon to teach the law in Israel, and leads the people in repenting of marriages that had drawn them toward the gods of the nations around them. In this translation he is called Esdras.

In the Greek Bible, Ezra and Nehemiah stand together as one book. Christians have long read the return and the rebuilding as a figure of the soul coming home from captivity and of the Church built up again, and early writers such as Saint Irenaeus honored Ezra as the scribe through whom God restored the Scriptures to His people after the exile.
