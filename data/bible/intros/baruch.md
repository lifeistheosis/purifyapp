Baruch, son of Neriah, was the faithful scribe who wrote down the prophecies of Jeremiah and shared his dangers. The book that bears his name is set among the exiles in Babylon. It opens with a long prayer of confession, in which the people own that their sins brought the exile upon them, then turns to a poem in praise of Wisdom, which God gave to Israel in the book of His commandments, and ends by comforting Jerusalem with the promise that her scattered children will come home.

The Church receives Baruch as belonging with Jeremiah, and Saint Athanasius and Saint Cyril of Jerusalem counted it with Jeremiah, Lamentations and the Epistle as a single book. Near the close of the poem on Wisdom stands a verse that the Fathers read as a clear prophecy of the Incarnation, that God appeared on earth and lived among men, and the passage is read at the services of the Nativity of Christ.
