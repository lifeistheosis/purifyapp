The First Epistle to the Thessalonians is among the earliest of Saint Paul's letters, written from Corinth about AD 51, only months after he had been forced to leave Thessalonica. He thanks God for a young church that had turned from idols to serve the living God and was standing firm under persecution, recalls how he had cared for them like a nursing mother, and urges them to holiness and quiet work. Some were grieving for members who had died before the Lord's coming, and Paul comforts them: those who have fallen asleep in Jesus will rise first, and the living and the dead will be with the Lord forever.

This passage on the departed is the Epistle read at every Orthodox funeral. The letter's call to unceasing prayer became for the hesychast Fathers the charter of the Jesus Prayer. Saint John Chrysostom preached eleven homilies on it.
