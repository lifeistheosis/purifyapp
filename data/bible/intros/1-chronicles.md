The two books of Chronicles are called Paralipomenon in the Greek Bible, the things left out, because they were taken to supply what the books of Kingdoms passed over. The first opens with nine chapters of genealogies, running from Adam through the tribes of Israel to the house of David. It then retells David's reign with a priest's eye: the bringing of the ark to Jerusalem with music and song, the ordering of the priests, the Levites and the singers, and David's gathering of gold, plans and craftsmen for the Temple that his son Solomon would build. It closes with David's great prayer of blessing before the assembly and the crowning of Solomon.

The Church reads Chronicles as a history centered on worship, in which the right praise of God stands at the heart of a people's life. The line of David traced here is the line Saint Matthew carries forward to Christ, the Son of David.
