Genesis (Greek: Γένεσις, "origin") is the first book of the Pentateuch - the five books of Moses that open the Old Testament. It begins with the creation of the world and the human person in the image of God, moves through the fall, the flood, and the calling of Abraham, and ends with the patriarchs in Egypt.

For the Orthodox Church, Genesis is read at every Vespers in Great Lent - its opening chapters frame the whole story of salvation that will culminate in Christ, the New Adam. The Fathers read its early chapters with both literal and typological care: Adam prefigures Christ; Eve, the Theotokos; the ark of Noah, the Church.

Brenton translates from the Greek Septuagint, the version of the Old Testament read by the Apostles and used by the Church to this day.
