Second Maccabees is not a sequel to the first book but a parallel account, condensed from a five-volume history by Jason of Cyrene. It runs from the days of the high priest Onias to the victory of Judas Maccabeus over Nicanor, and it dwells on the Temple, on God's care for it, and on those who died rather than break His law. Here are the aged scribe Eleazar, who would not even pretend to eat forbidden meat, and the seven brothers tortured to death one after another, strengthened by their mother, who confessed that God made all things from nothing and will raise the dead.

The Church honors these Holy Maccabean Martyrs on August 1, and Saint Gregory the Theologian and Saint John Chrysostom preached in their praise. The book is also an early witness to prayer for the departed, in Judas's offering for his fallen soldiers, and to the prayers of the saints for the living.
