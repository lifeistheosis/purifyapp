The Wisdom of Solomon belongs to the deuterocanonical books that the Eastern Church receives as Scripture and the Septuagint hands down to us. Written in Greek by an unknown Hellenistic Jewish author around the first century before Christ, it speaks in the voice of King Solomon to the philosophers of his age, defending the worship of the one God against the empty wisdom of idols.

The book moves in three movements. The first contrasts the destiny of the righteous and the godless and includes the great passage on the souls of the just being in the hand of God, a verse the Orthodox Church reads at every funeral and on the feast of every righteous person. The second is a long meditation on Wisdom herself, the radiance of God's glory, the unblemished mirror of His working, the friend of God and the prophets. The third retells the Exodus as a parable of how God saves His own and patiently chastens the rest.

For the Fathers Wisdom prefigures Christ. The Wisdom of God in chapter seven and eight became, with Proverbs eight, a charter for early Christology, drawn on by Athanasius and Cyril of Alexandria against the Arians. Wisdom is read at Vespers of the great feasts and of the saints, and on Holy Saturday at the morning Liturgy.
