Paul's First Epistle to the Corinthians is the apostle's longest sustained address to a single church, written from Ephesus around the year 54 to a young and quarrelsome community in the trading capital of Greece. They had divided into factions, tolerated grave sin, dragged each other into court, abused the Lord's Supper, and disputed about gifts and the resurrection. Paul answers each, point by point, and turns every question into a teaching about Christ and the Cross.

The thirteenth chapter, the great hymn of love, is read at every Orthodox wedding and at most funerals. The eleventh chapter records the earliest written account of the Lord's Supper. The fifteenth opens with the apostolic kerygma in its briefest form, that Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose again on the third day according to the Scriptures, and unfolds the doctrine of the bodily resurrection on which the whole gospel stands.

The Church reads Corinthians continuously at the Liturgy throughout the long stretch of weeks after Pentecost. Saint John Chrysostom devoted 44 homilies to the letter; in them he treats not only the Corinthians' problems but every parish that has ever read them.
