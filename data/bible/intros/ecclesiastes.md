Ecclesiastes, from the Greek for one who speaks to the assembly, gives the reflections of the Preacher, son of David and king in Jerusalem, whom tradition identifies with Solomon. He has tried wisdom, pleasure, great works and wealth, and found all of it vanity, a breath that passes. Generations come and go, the same end meets the wise and the fool, and every matter under the sun has its appointed time. Yet he counsels joy in the simple gifts of God, and ends by calling his reader to fear God and keep His commandments.

The Fathers read the book as a teacher of detachment from what passes away. Saint Gregory of Nyssa, who preached eight homilies on it, saw Solomon's three books as one path: Proverbs trains the beginner in virtue, Ecclesiastes loosens the soul from visible things, and the Song of Songs leads it into union with God.
