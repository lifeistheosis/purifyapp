Zephaniah, whose descent the book traces to Hezekiah, probably the king of that name, prophesied in the days of King Josiah of Judah. He announces the day of the Lord as a day of wrath and distress, of darkness and the sound of the trumpet, sweeping away the idolatry of Jerusalem along with the pride of the surrounding nations. Yet beyond the judgment he sees a humble and lowly people left in the land who will fear the name of the Lord, and he calls the daughter of Zion to sing and rejoice with all her heart, for the Lord her King is in her midst.

The Church reads that call to joy at Vespers of Palm Sunday, as Christ enters Jerusalem as King, and another passage from the same chapter at the Vesperal Liturgy of Holy Saturday. In this translation the prophet is called Sophonias.
