Saint Paul wrote to the Philippians from prison, to the first church he had founded in Europe, a community that had supported him with gifts from the beginning. It is the warmest of his letters and the letter of joy. Though in chains and facing possible death, he rejoices, counts everything as loss beside knowing Christ, and presses on toward the prize of God's upward call. He urges his readers to rejoice in the Lord always, to bring every care to God in prayer, and to fill their minds with whatever is true, honorable and pure.

At its heart is the hymn of Christ's self-emptying, in which the eternal Son takes the form of a servant, humbles Himself even to death on the Cross, and is exalted above every name. The Church reads this passage at the feasts of the Theotokos, who in her humility bore the One who emptied Himself. Saint John Chrysostom preached fifteen homilies on the letter.
