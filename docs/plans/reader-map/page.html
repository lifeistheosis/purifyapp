The questions, as a reader meets them

Three questions decide everything on this page. The second is asked in the reader's own terms, so it has three forms.

How the answers choose the road

By the first answer, then the second. Where two roads are named, the first pick decides: the Fathers, the councils or Church history make a seeker.

Why this page looks as it does

One number leads: the eight roads. It is the one thing to hold in the head. Everything else on the page hangs from it.

Every picture is the app's own screen. Taken from the app as readers have it, cut across its whole width, with nothing drawn over it. So what you judge is what a reader sees.

Each road is seven rows in one tray. It is the house's form for a list of places to go, and each row opens the real page, so a road can be walked from here.

The roads are a list of rows, not pills. Eight destinations, each with a line that says who is on it.

The numbers are rows, not tiles. Five figures that read down a phone in the order they matter.

No chart. Nothing here is a trend, so nothing is drawn as one.

Dark, phone first, no parchment. It is read on a phone, in the app's own colours.
