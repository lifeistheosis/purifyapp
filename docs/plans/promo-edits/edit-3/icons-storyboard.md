How icons move: an icon is never redrawn (CLAUDE.md), so no icon is bent in depth as the paintings were. The move allowed is a figure cut out whole and moved over its own gold, a little larger than itself so nothing behind it is ever invented, with the camera moving at a steady speed.

Named to him: The Met holds the Annunciation and the Entry into Jerusalem only in black-and-white photographs; and no Crucifixion or Resurrection icon was found in full resolution, so this story is the Incarnation, the Mother and the Child, the Entry its last scene.

## His word on it: "better clear high quality easter icons (nativity, etc)"

So the black-and-white photographs go, and the feasts come in clear colour at full resolution from the Russian Museum's zoom viewer (`rusmuseumvrm.ru/fullimages/dz_canvas/drz-<n>/dzc_output.xml`, put together with the scratchpad's `dzi.py`). Found (catalogue numbers, ДРЖ): the Meeting in the Temple, Andrei Rublev, about 1408, from the festal row of the Dormition Cathedral in Vladimir (2135); the Descent into Hades, the Pascha icon, Dionisy and workshop, about 1502 to 1503 (3094); the Annunciation, 16th century (1375); the Baptism, Stroganov school (1020); the Nativity, early 16th century (1248, already fetched); the Raising of Lazarus, Novgorod, about 1497 (2784); the Dormition, Novgorod, 15th century (2065, 2066); a Crucifixion (1219); the Transfiguration with the feasts round it (1879).

His next word, on seeing the museum icons: "no HIGH quality recent ones". So not the old panels either (worn faces, cracked gold): icons painted in our own time, crisp and bright, by Orthodox iconographers, at full resolution. His rule of the same day allows it ("a modern icon, a photograph or a film still may be used" in an edit). The museum files stay in `.promo/edit-3/raw/feasts/` (the Vault rule).

He sent the look he means ("like this"): a modern Greek icon of the Resurrection (the Descent into Hades) titled in English, "THE RESURRECTION OF CHRIST": clean drawn line, bright clear colour, a deep blue night sky, Christ in white in a starred blue mandorla raising Adam and Eve. Kept as `.promo/edit-3/raw/modern/his_reference_resurrection.jpg`. So the feasts are sought in that style, one hand or one workshop for all of them if it can be found, at full size.

## Storyboard 2 (waiting on his yes)

The sheet is `.promo/edit-3/choose/icons-storyboard-2.jpg`, drawn by `icons_board.py` from the files themselves: every panel is a crop of the icon, scaled, nothing drawn.

**The icons.** Eight great feasts by one hand, Nikolaos Galanopoulos, a Greek iconographer of our day (each signed "Χείρ Νικ. Γαλανόπουλου", the hand of Nik. Galanopoulos), sold as printed icons by Athonite USA (athoniteusa.com; each product page and file is in `.promo/edit-3/raw/modern/athonite/sources.json`). The files are the prints' own masters, 2362 by 3307 pixels (20 by 28 cm at 300 dpi), clean new paint with Greek titles: the same modern Greek school as the Resurrection he sent. One hand for every feast gives the film one look from the first beat to the last.

What it does for the viewer: the whole Gospel in fifty-six seconds, told in the Church's own feasts, each a picture a viewer knows; the song's shape carries the story (build, drop, stop, silence, return). The look: bright new icons, gold ground, the whisper's cuts on every beat and every hi-hat, crush and develop between feasts. The goal: an edit that reads as Orthodox at first sight and premium in every frame, with nothing redrawn.

**Left out, and why.** The Transfiguration (his set's ninth feast): its painted light is a burst of beams from Christ, the look the no-rays rule names. Christ of the Ascension close: a burst of gold rays behind Him, so the last look into the lens is the Mother's. The heaven at the top of the Annunciation, the Nativity and the Baptism (rays round a half circle) stays out of frame. The museum icons (worn), and TheHolyArt's (product photographs with a watermark).

**Named to him.** The Resurrection's glory round Christ has faint painted rays among its stars (panels 9 and 10): the icon's own painting, which is never drawn over. If he wants no rays at all on screen, the Resurrection can be the one he sent (stars, no rays), at its lower size (1024 by 1553), or the Resurrection is kept wide only.

**Sharpness.** The closest shots (panels 5 and 11) are 2.25 times the print file, sharp at phone size; closer than that the gold shows the file's compression, so no shot goes closer.

**Rights.** A living iconographer's work, sold as prints. As an edit, an unpaid post on Purify's own accounts, his rule of 2026-11-25 allows it, with the risk he accepted (a takedown on a rights holder's request). A paid advert would need permission first. The caption should credit him: "Icons: Nikolaos Galanopoulos".

How the icons move is as above: crops and steady camera moves, a figure at most cut out whole; no depth bending, no bounce.
