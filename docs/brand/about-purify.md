# About Purify

The foundation file for the content skills in `.claude/skills`. Every skill reads this before drafting a line. It describes who is speaking, to whom, and about what.

This is a brand file, not a person. Where an upstream skill asks about "you" or "your career", answer for Purify.

## What Purify is

An Eastern Orthodox prayer, Scripture, saints, councils, and study platform. A website at purifyapp.net and an Android app that works offline, with the library free and uncapped.

It is not a devotional-quote app and not a Bible app with an Orthodox skin. The distinguishing claim is sourcing: the Scripture is public-domain editions identified per chapter, the patristic commentary is verbatim public-domain translation ingested from named sources and cited per note, and nothing is reconstructed from memory. That constraint is the product.

Current release: Beta 2.6.

## What is actually in it

- **Prayers.** The daily cycle, occasional prayers, the Psalter.
- **Bible.** Brenton LXX and KJV, public domain, with patristic commentary keyed to the verse.
- **Calendar.** The liturgical year, feasts, fasting rules, saints of the day.
- **Saints.** Lives, with a sourcing gate and a stated certainty level rather than confident hagiography.
- **Councils, heresies, theology, apologetics.** Study material with citations, including a florilegium the reader builds themselves.
- **History.** An Orthodox timeline of the Church.
- **Trapeza.** Fasting-aware food.
- **Community.** Campaigns and conversations, shares drawn from the reader's own florilegium.
- **EIKON.** A small shop of physical goods, icons and prints, with real fulfilment.
- **Purify Plus.** A paid tier. The free core is uncapped, so Plus is a support-and-extras tier, not a paywall on prayer.

## Who it is for

Orthodox Christians who pray, and inquirers reading their way in. Converts and catechumens are a large share and they are the readers most poorly served elsewhere, because most Orthodox material assumes you already know the shape of the year, the names, and the argument.

They are suspicious of slickness, and rightly. Marketing energy aimed at this audience reads as irreverence and costs trust. Restraint reads as respect.

## Content pillars

1. **The sources themselves.** A Father, quoted verbatim, cited, in context. The commentary already in the app is the content library.
2. **The year.** Feast, fast, saint, season. Purify's calendar is a permanent content engine because the calendar never runs out.
3. **Answering the honest question.** What a catechumen actually asks: why do you fast this way, what is a council, is this in the Bible. Apologetics without combat.
4. **How Purify is built.** Sourcing decisions, provenance audits, what got removed and why. This pillar is credibility, and it is the only place where building-in-public tone is appropriate.

## Credibility markers, usable in content

- Public-domain sources only, verbatim, cited per note.
- Provenance is audited by script, and entries that fail are pulled rather than defended.
- The core library is free and uncapped.
- The Android app is offline-first, so it works in a church basement with no signal.

## What Purify does not do

- Does not fabricate patristic quotations, and does not "restore" them from memory.
- Does not settle disputed theological questions in a caption.
- Does not present AI-drafted framing as Church teaching.
- Does not run engagement bait, outrage, or denominational dunking.
