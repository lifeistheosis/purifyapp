## What the full film would take

At 1.9 seconds a frame, 30 frames a second: a film of 75 seconds is 2250 frames and about 1.2 hours; 90 seconds is 2700 frames and about 1.4 hours. The first scene alone took 2.54 seconds a frame; the second is lighter (one cloud of points, no map once it has gone dark), so the average fell. A background job can be given up to two hours (`timeout`), and `--film --resume --minutes=N` keeps what a stopped run left: each frame is the same whenever it is drawn.

## The numerals' face

**Archivo Narrow**, weight 600, by Omnibus-Type, SIL Open Font Licence 1.1, from `@fontsource/archivo-narrow`. One face for every figure in the film: the large figures, the six shares, the share in scene two and the 100 in the key. The words keep DM Serif Display and DM Sans.

- **Lining and one width, as drawn.** Measured in the page before any frame: each of the ten digits is 45.6 wide at a size of 100. No font feature is switched on; a canvas cannot switch one on, which is why a face that is tabular by default was needed. `render.mjs --check` refuses the face if the ten widths differ.
- **What was passed over, by the same measure.** Barlow Condensed (digits 26.6 to 44.7 wide), Big Shoulders Display (21.8 to 41.7), Saira Condensed (28.3 to 49.0), Sofia Sans Extra Condensed (31.7 to 38.7) and Oswald (38.2 to 53.4) all draw a narrower 1 by default, so a running count would shiver in them.
- **Why this one.** A narrow upright grotesque is a film's figure (a lower third, a counter), not a book's; narrow lets six digits and a comma be set at 196 px in a square frame (digits about 135 px tall).

## How it was made

`shot.js` is both scenes as one three.js scene and one camera (WebGL2, in software through SwiftShader), set to any moment by frame number. `render.mjs` checks the rules and the measures, asks the page for one frame at a time, and ffmpeg writes them with a fine grain (a fixed seed). `words.mjs` holds every word and figure on screen.

- **Scene one is unchanged.** Everything scene two adds to the camera, the lens and the map is nothing, and at rest, until 10.0 seconds (`GO`); the last frame of scene one is drawn from times before it. Only the figures' face differs from the first sample.
- **The hand-over, with no cut.** At 10.0 the drift the camera already has becomes one move of 2.2 seconds: it turns 100 degrees about the country, rises 3 map units with the points it follows, and lowers its angle from 38 to 36 degrees. The move is eased from rest, so it begins at the drift's own speed. The six forms sink in 0.9 seconds and their light goes in 0.6; the map's own lattice brightens, then the whole map goes down to nothing by 12.0. There is one scene and one camera throughout; nothing is mixed or faded between two pictures.
- **The points.** One for every 100 people: 6,758 for 675,765 and 1,830 for 183,020 (rounded). 3,238 leave from the tops of the six forms, each state giving its share of the points (California 737, New York 723, Illinois 514, Florida 433, New Jersey 419, Pennsylvania 412); no other state has a checked figure, so the other 3,520 leave evenly from the other states. Each goes to the place in the field that keeps its order among the others (west stays west), so paths do not cross.
- **The field.** A lattice of equal triangles, the 6,758 sites nearest the middle: a round field 4.5 map units in reach, 3 units over the map.
- **Who stays.** 1,830 points drawn by a fixed dice from the whole field, the same on every render: the source parts regular attendees by no place. The others sink and go out in a wave from the far edge to the near one (1.0 second across, each up to 0.5 late, 0.5 to fall).
- **The count.** The figure is the points standing in the field, as a share of the way between the two checked figures: it runs up to 675,765 as the last point arrives (12.0), holds, runs down from 14.2 and reads 183,020 at 16.2. Seven fixed cells, so nothing moves sideways.
- **Drawing together.** Those who stay move to the 1,830 innermost sites of the same lattice, each to the free site nearest where a plain drawing in would put it. Same spacing as the full field, so the body's area against the ring's is the share. They brighten past white as they do, which is the scene's only glow.
- **The ring.** A line 0.024 units wide at the edge of the full field, from 14.4. It is a measure, not an ornament: it keeps the size of the rolls on screen.
- **Blur and focus.** Each frame is the scene drawn 20 to 72 times within its own shutter (20 at rest, up to 56 in the hand-over), each drawing a moment later and through a different point of the lens, averaged in linear light.
- **Scene one's method** (the cut inside the push, height as the figure, the globe and the map) is as the first sample's record gave it: `git log` on this file.
- **Faces.** Archivo Narrow for figures; DM Serif Display and DM Sans, the app's own, for words. All from open-licence packages (`@fontsource`).
- **Light.** A soft glow at the globe's rim, on the top of each risen form and on those who stay. No rays, flare, streak or sparkle; every point is a round point.
- **The house's rules.** `render.mjs --check` holds `shot.js`, `shot.html` and `words.mjs` to the measure of `lib/marketing/__tests__/promoLooks.test.ts` before any frame (no conic gradient, no sparkle glyph), and also refuses rays, flares and streaks by their 3D names, a dash in place of words, parchment or sepia, a borrowed picture, and a colour that is not a grey.
- **The measures.** On rendered frames of both scenes with the words taken out, the brightest ground behind each piece of words (the 99th percentile of its box) is held to 4.5:1 against the word, every piece must be inside the frame, and no letter may be under 30 px (10.8 on a phone 390 wide). Result: checked: 43 readings of 12 pieces of words, least contrast 4.67:1, smallest letters 30px; 10 figures held to facts.mjs; the ten digits of Archivo Narrow each 45.6 wide at 100; 6758 points, 1830 stay, 3238 leave from the six states; ANGLE (Google, Vulkan 1.3.0 (SwiftShader Device (Subzero) (0x0000C0DE)), SwiftShader driver)
- **checkMock.** It measures an app mockup. A film frame has no palette and no controls, so the frames are held by `render.mjs --check`; the page he sees it on is held by checkMock itself (`page.mjs --check`, five palettes at 360, 390, 412 and 1032).
- **Pictures.** None. `lib/marketing/heritagePictures.ts` is not touched.

## The figures

All from [U.S. Religion Census 2020](https://www.usreligioncensus.org/sites/default/files/2023-03/Appendix_F--Orthodox_Christian_Groups.pdf), Appendix F: Orthodox Christian Groups, by Alexei Krindatch, read again on 2026-11-13 for this pass.

- 47.9% and the six shares: p. 4: "nearly half of all U.S. Eastern Orthodox Church adherents (47.9%) lived in six states: California (10.9%), New York (10.7%), Illinois (7.6%), Florida (6.4%), New Jersey (6.2%), and Pennsylvania (6.1%) ... only 34.8% of the total U.S. population lives in these six states."
- 675,765: p. 3, as above. An adherent is anyone taking part in a parish's life, "regular and occasional attendees", adults and children (p. 1).
- 183,020: p. 3: "The number of regular attendees was 183,020 in the Eastern 171,045 in the Oriental Orthodox Churches." Attendees are those at Liturgy "on a typical (not festive) Sunday" (p. 1).
- 27%: p. 3: 183,020 regular attendees and 675,765 adherents in the Eastern Orthodox Churches. 183,020 divided by 675,765 is 0.2708. The measure is the source's own (p. 3: "percentage of adherents who are regular attendees"), which it prints for single Churches and not for all of them together; the division for all of them is ours. It is in `../facts.mjs` as `DERIVED`, apart from `FACTS`, because the first film's check holds every figure in `FACTS` to its own page.

## To make it again

`npm install three world-atlas us-atlas topojson-client d3-geo @fontsource/dm-serif-display @fontsource/dm-sans @fontsource/archivo-narrow` in `.promo/orthodox-america-3d/tools`, then `render.mjs --check`, `--stills <seconds> [--strip]`, `--draft` or `--film` (with Playwright on `NODE_PATH`). To grow it into the film: each new scene adds its own time constants that are nothing until its own start, as scene two's do after `GO`, and its own words in `words.mjs` held to `facts.mjs`.
