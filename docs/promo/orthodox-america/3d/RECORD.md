## The outro

- **Names, not logos.** No logo, mark or shield of any source. Pew's terms of use do not permit use of its marks; Harvard's Trademark Program requires written permission; a reel of logos reads as endorsement. Each source is a card in the film's type, in the order the film used it.
- **Harvard is not named.** The Orthodox Studies Institute's report (read with pdftotext, 2026-11-13 20:40 UTC) never names Harvard or any university for the Cooperative Election Study. By the brief, the card does not either. `render.mjs --check` refuses a card that names a university.
- **The Cooperative Election Study's card is earned:** scene five shows its median ages (Orthodox 48; Catholics 58, Evangelicals 59, Mainline Protestants 63), as the Institute's report gives them.
- **The mark.** `words.mjs` MARK holds the four rectangles of `components/ui/PurifyMark.tsx` number for number (its 100 by 176 box, each bar's place, size and corner, the footrest's turn of 18 degrees about 50, 138), and `render.mjs --check` reads the component and refuses a difference. They are thin slabs, 400 px tall at rest, seen square on when the camera settles. The post rises, the titulus and the crossbar open from the middle, the footrest opens flat and takes its slant last. "Purify" in DM Serif Display. A soft pool of light behind; no rays, flare or sparkle.
- **The church's cross and the mark are never one object.** Scene three's church keeps the plain cross of the Greek tradition.

## Growth: what the film says and what it does not

- **What it says.** In a survey of 270 Orthodox parishes (Faith Communities Today, December 2025, as Alexei Krindatch reports it in September 2026), attendance in the median parish rose 36% from 2019 to 2025 while U.S. congregations as a whole did not change; by jurisdiction 87, 67, 60 and 12%. Read from the report's PDF with pdftotext on 2026-11-13. The last scene turns on it, after the decade's fall to 2020, and ends on the report's own sentence.
- **What it does not say.** That the Orthodox share of Americans has grown. A research pass on 2026-11-13 read Pew's 2025 report (1% in 2007, under 1% in 2014, 1% in 2023 to 2024) and PRRI's 2025 Census of American Religion ("little growth among Orthodox Christians (0.5% in both 2013 and 2025)"). The film claims nothing about that share.
- **Not used.** The Orthodox Studies Institute's estimate of 16,000 to 18,000 converts at Pascha 2026 was found only in a news summary (orthochristian.com), not on the Institute's own pages. The OCA's net gain of 20 parishes from 2020 to 2024 is one jurisdiction. Newspaper reports of full churches are accounts, not counts.
- **The arbiter's note on checkpoint 1:** the title's small line is in sentence case with no added tracking ("By the numbers"), as the design kit requires.

## The figures, each with its line in facts.mjs

- **47.9% in six states.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "where", shows 47.9. U.S. Religion Census 2020: p. 4: "nearly half of all U.S. Eastern Orthodox Church adherents (47.9%) lived in six states: California (10.9%), New York (10.7%), Illinois (7.6%), Florida (6.4%), New Jersey (6.2%), and Pennsylvania (6.1%) ... only 34.8% of the total U.S. population lives in these six states."
- **675,765 on the rolls.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "gap", shows 675765. U.S. Religion Census 2020: p. 3, as above. An adherent is anyone taking part in a parish's life, "regular and occasional attendees", adults and children (p. 1).
- **183,020 on a typical Sunday.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "gap", shows 183020. U.S. Religion Census 2020: p. 3: "The number of regular attendees was 183,020 in the Eastern 171,045 in the Oriental Orthodox Churches." Attendees are those at Liturgy "on a typical (not festive) Sunday" (p. 1).
- **27% of the rolls.** facts.mjs DERIVED, shows 27%. U.S. Religion Census 2020: p. 3: 183,020 regular attendees and 675,765 adherents in the Eastern Orthodox Churches. 183,020 divided by 675,765 is 0.2708. The measure is the source's own (p. 3: "percentage of adherents who are regular attendees"), which it prints for single Churches and not for all of them together; the division for all of them is ours.
- **2,014 parishes, and 2,979 in each.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "six", shows 2,979. U.S. Religion Census 2020: p. 3: "2,014 Eastern Orthodox and 895 Oriental Orthodox parishes." 6,000,000 divided by 2,014 is 2,979. The division is ours.
- **"more than 6,000,000 citizens", 1942.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "six", shows 6000000. Matthew Namee: "In March 1942, the primates of seven American Orthodox jurisdictions issued a proclamation that began, '... more than 6,000,000 citizens…'" It was part of a lobbying effort to be recognised by the government in wartime. "There never has been any basis for the claim that six million Americans are or were Orthodox."
- **130 in the median parish, 60 on a Sunday.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "six", shows 130. Alexei Krindatch: p. 8: "Change in the size of a median parish measured by number of total adherents: 150 to 130 (-13%)"; by regular attendees, "60 to 60 (no change)".
- **The four ages.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "who", shows 24%. Pew Research Center: "24% are between the ages of 18 and 29, 35% are ages 30 to 49, 23% are ages 50 to 65 and 17% are 65 and older." "Margin of error for figures based on Orthodox Christians is ±9.9 percentage points."
- **The median ages.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "who", shows Median age 48. Matthew Namee and Dn. Seraphim Richard Rohlin: p. 9: "With a median age of 48, the Orthodox CES respondents were significantly younger than every other Christian group: compare to median ages of 58 for Catholics, 59 for Evangelicals, and 63 for Mainline Protestants."
- **64% of those raised Orthodox.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "who", shows data-count="64". Matthew Namee and Dn. Seraphim Richard Rohlin: p. 34: "a retention rate for cradle Orthodox: 64% ... Basically, six in ten Americans who grew up Orthodox still identify as Orthodox."
- **24% raised in another tradition.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "who", shows data-count="24". Matthew Namee and Dn. Seraphim Richard Rohlin: p. 36: "people who are Orthodox now, but who were raised in some other tradition. In the Pew data, 24% of current Orthodox people are converts."
- **53% men.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "who", shows data-count="53". Matthew Namee and Dn. Seraphim Richard Rohlin: p. 6: "Orthodoxy was 53% male in Pew, and 55% male in CES. Every other Christian group was majority female."
- **40% immigrants, another 25% their children.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "who", shows data-count="40". Matthew Namee and Dn. Seraphim Richard Rohlin: p. 3: "In Pew, a remarkable 40% of Orthodox Christians reported being immigrants, and another 25% were the children of immigrants." (CES found just under 20% immigrants.)
- **63% college graduates.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "who", shows data-count="63". Matthew Namee and Dn. Seraphim Richard Rohlin: p. 21: "according to Pew, 35% hold graduate degrees ... When you throw in 4-year degrees, 63% are college graduates. No other Christian group comes close."
- **The four regions.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "where", shows 31%. Pew Research Center: "19% of this group lives in the Midwest, 29% live in the Northeast, 31% live in the South and 21% live in the West."
- **2010 to 2020.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "change", shows 816,653. Alexei Krindatch: p. 5: Eastern Orthodox adherents "dropped from 816,653 to 675,765 (-17%), and their number of regular attendees declined from 212,212 to 183,020 (-14%). Oriental Orthodox Churches have grown from 294,762 to 491,413 total adherents (+67%)."
- **Converts received, 2013 to 2023.** facts.mjs FACTS, scene "change", shows [2013, 91]. Matthew Namee and others: Table 1, p. 10: 91, 91, 86, 88, 88, 90, 104, 59, 100, 186, 124 for 2013 to 2023. "20 parishes across 6 different jurisdictions in 15 states"; 2023 is a part year (p. 1).
- **Attendance up 36% since 2019, and the closing sentence.** facts.mjs LATER, shows 36%. Alexei Krindatch: p. 7: "On a typical Sunday, US religious congregations as a whole are seeing the same number of worshippers today as they did before the pandemic. In contrast, Orthodox parishes experienced a significant gain (+36%) in attendance. Simply put, a 36% growth means that for every three people praying on Sunday in a typical American Orthodox parish before the pandemic, there are now four." Fig. 7, 2019 to 2025 change in median worship attendance at regular weekend services: Orthodox Christian 36%, Antiochian Archdiocese 87%, Greek Orthodox 12%, Orthodox Church in America 67%, Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia 60%. p. 2: the Faith Communities Today survey of December 2025, "7,500 congregations across 79 denominations and faith traditions ... including 270 Orthodox Christian parishes."
