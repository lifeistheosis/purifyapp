In the United States, approximately 1% of adults (around 2.6 million people) self-identify as Orthodox Christians. However, official church-reported census figures show significantly fewer active participants, highlighting a large gap between cultural self-identification and regular parish attendance. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_North_America), [2] (https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/religious-tradition/orthodox-christian/), [3] (https://www.usreligioncensus.org/sites/default/files/2023-03/Appendix_F--Orthodox_Christian_Groups.pdf)

Demographics & Education

According to data from the Pew Research Center Religious Landscape Study and the Orthodox Studies Institute, American Orthodox Christians stand out in several demographic categories:
• Age distribution. The community skews younger than many mainline Christian groups, with 24% aged 18–29, 35% aged 30–49, 23% aged 50–65, and 17% aged 65 and older. [1] (https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/religious-tradition/orthodox-christian/), [2] (https://www.orthodoxstudies.org/documents/11/american-orthodoxy-today-2025.pdf)
• Gender and background. American Orthodoxy has a higher proportion of men and immigrants compared to the general U.S. Christian population. [1] (https://www.orthodoxstudies.org/documents/11/american-orthodoxy-today-2025.pdf)
• Converts vs. cradle Orthodox. Roughly 6 in 10 individuals raised in the Orthodox faith remain in the church as adults, while about 25% of current Orthodox Christians in America are adult converts. [1] (https://www.orthodoxstudies.org/documents/11/american-orthodoxy-today-2025.pdf)
• Education and income. Statistically, Eastern Orthodox Christians are among the highest-earning and most educated religious groups in the U.S., with high rates of college and post-graduate degrees. [1] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_North_America)

Recent Growth & Trends

• A split reality. Between 2010 and 2020, total reported Eastern Orthodox adherents declined by 17% largely due to aging populations in traditional, ethnically anchored parishes.
• Convert-driven surge. Since 2020, English-language and convert-friendly parishes—especially in Sun Belt metros like Dallas, Phoenix, Nashville, Austin, and Charlotte—have experienced rapid growth.
• Newcomer profile. Recent convert surges are heavily driven by young adults aged 18–35 (particularly young men) and young families, with roughly half coming from Evangelical backgrounds and a quarter each from Catholicism and non-religious backgrounds.
