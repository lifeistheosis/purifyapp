# December's saints (1.9): the plan

**Opened.** 2026-11-10, on the arbiter's word ("Then December, with the same
brief as November"), the owner's of 2026-11-10 carried on: every saint the
month commemorates shown, with a page, his Life, his works and his links, done
the same way each month and ready before the month's first day.
**The path.** docs/editorial/menologion-month.md, as November left it.
**Binding.** docs/editorial-standards.md: public-domain sources only,
verbatim, cited per passage; no life of ours; no AI-drafted framing shipped.

`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/menologion-month.mjs plan december`
reads the calendar (data/calendar/daily-saints.json).

## The counts

- **57 lines**: 50 saints' lines and 7 feasts.
- **7 saints' lines with a page** already, all written before November (a life
  of ours, kept and reported, never failed): St Cleopas of Sihăstria, St
  Porphyrios, St John of Damascus, St Daniel the Stylite, St Spyridon, St
  Herman of Alaska, St Ignatius of Antioch.
- **3 more lines whose page exists but is not linked** from the calendar: St
  Ambrose (December 7), St Peter of Moscow (December 21) and the feast of St
  Stephen the Protomartyr (December 27). Their lines get their slugs.
- **41 lines need a page.** The feasts: St Nicholas's opens his page, the
  Synaxis of the Theotokos and her Conception open hers, St Stephen's opens
  his; the three of the Nativity are feasts of the Lord and stay as they are.
  The icon "Unexpected Joy" is a feast of an icon: it opens the Theotokos's
  page, with no page of its own.
- **Lives in a witness's words** for 12 of the 41 new lines and for 3 pages
  that exist: Scripture for 7 lines (five prophets, the apostles of the
  Seventy, the Holy Innocents) and for St Stephen's page; the historians and
  the chronicles for 5 (the Martyrs of Nicomedia, St Melania, St Savva of
  Storozhevsk, St Nilus of Stolobny, and St Modestus, to confirm) and for St
  Ambrose's and St Peter of Moscow's pages. The rest have an entry with its
  source.
- **Butler only, on his word**: about 15 saints whose only public-domain
  English life is Alban Butler's (below), to be held for the priest as
  November's eight are, if he says yes.
- **Works**: St Ambrose's (NPNF II 10, 1896), St John of Damascus's On Holy
  Images with his three sermons on the Dormition (tr. Mary H. Allies, 1898),
  the books of the five prophets (Brenton's Septuagint, the app's own Bible),
  and, on his word, St John of Kronstadt's My Life in Christ (tr. E. E.
  Goulaeff, 1897).

The Old Calendar keeps these days thirteen days later (December 25 is January
7), so for readers on it the month runs to mid-January; the pages carry the
calendar's day as the calendar gives it, as November's do.

## Needs his word

1. **Butler's Lives for the Butler-only saints**, held for a priest exactly as
   November's eight (word for word, his name on each, his passages on Roman
   doctrine left out and the cut shown, one clergy item): St Barbara, St
   Sabbas, St Lucy, St Anastasia, St Eugenia, St Theodore the Branded, St
   Marcellus and St Anysia in his December volume; St Sebastian, St Juliana,
   St Chrysogonus, Sts Thyrsus, Leucius and Callinicus and St Boniface in
   others; St Eleutherius, Sts Philemon and Apollonius, Sts Eustratius and
   the Ten Martyrs of Crete if he has them (to confirm). Rec: yes, the same
   way. Until his word they have the short entry.
2. **St John of Kronstadt's My Life in Christ** (tr. E. E. Goulaeff, 1897,
   public domain): his own words, but no one has transcribed the printing,
   and the whole book typed from its scans is more than three days. Rec: a
   selection now (about 50 pages from the start, typed and checked, about a
   day), the rest later.

Nothing else needs his word: every other source is public domain, there is
no framing for the clergy queue, and the size and time are under the bounds.

1. Push 1: the 41 pages with their entries and sources, the calendar's links

## Push 1, as built (2026-11-10)

`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/menologion-month.mjs check december`:
every saint's line has a page, and every new page meets the standard.
December is in DONE_MONTHS (lib/saints/month.ts), and the month test holds it
there.

- **40 new pages**, not 41 as planned: the line of the icon "Unexpected Joy"
  opens the Theotokos's page and needed none. Each holds a short entry of
  plain facts (at most 80 words) and names its sources under it. The facts
  were read by a helper from the Dictionary of Christian Biography, The Book
  of Saints, Baring-Gould, Dunbar, Mouravieff and the translator's preface to
  My Life in Christ (1897), each quote checked on the printed page, and the
  entries were written from those quotes and the calendar's own lines only. Where the books disagree (St
  Barbara's place, St Sabbas's years, St Lucy's death, St Eugenia's two
  stories, St Menas's calling) the entry says so rather than choosing. Where a
  source differs from the Church's own account (the Dictionary, after
  Zonaras, makes St Philaret the father of the empress Maria; his Life makes
  him her grandfather), or from itself (St Modestus's years), the entry says
  only what both bear; St Eleutherius's Acts are named as Baring-Gould doubts
  them.
- **Five saints have nothing in public-domain English**: St Joasaph of
  Belgorod, St Luke the New Stylite, St Stephen of Surozh, St Elias of Murom
  and St Niphon of Cyprus. Their pages hold what the calendar keeps, and say
  that no account of their lives in public-domain English has been found.
  St Philaret the Merciful has one passing mention (the Dictionary of
  Christian Biography, after Zonaras) and St Savva and St Nilus of Stolobny
  one or two sentences of Mouravieff each; their entries say no more.
- **The Lives in the witnesses' own words**
  (`scripts/menologion/december-lives.mjs`, review in
  `docs/editorial/ingest-review/december-lives.md`): the five prophets'
  books whole (Brenton's Septuagint, the app's Bible; Daniel with Susanna and
  Bel); every verse that names Sosthenes, Apollos and Tychicus; the Holy
  Innocents (Matthew 2:13-18); St Stephen (Acts 6:1 to 8:2); the Martyrs of
  Nicomedia (Eusebius VIII.6); St Melania (Palladius LXI, Clarke 1918); St
  Ambrose (Socrates IV.30, St Augustine's Confessions V.13, VI.3 and IX.7,
  Sozomen VII.25, Theodoret V.17); St Peter of Moscow (Mouravieff's chapter,
  pp. 51 to 54, typed from the page images). Every passage read against the
  scans; nothing open.
  - St Melania's text is the page's, not the transcription's: "thou" where
    the transcription has "thon", the footnotes' numbers out, its section
    numbers kept as the book's other chapters in the app keep them (a new
    `sectionNumbers` switch in the ingest).
  - Socrates IV.30 in CCEL lost the page's opening quotation marks and a
    comma; they are put back from the page (pp. 113 and 114).
  - **For his word**: Mouravieff says the khan and the Mongols "had lately

## Push 2a, as built: the works (2026-11-10)

`scripts/menologion/december-works.mjs`, read by `node
scripts/ingest-witness-lives.mjs december --works`; every word read against
two scans of its printing, the review in
`docs/editorial/ingest-review/december-works.md`. Six works whole, about
193,000 words.

- **St Ambrose**, from the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series,
  vol. X (1896): On the Duties of the Clergy (three books, 102 chapters), On
  the Holy Spirit (three books, 53 sections with his own prologues to the
  first two), On the Mysteries (nine chapters) and Concerning Repentance (two
  books, 28 chapters). The volume prints an editor's summary under every
  chapter heading; those are left out, and his numbered paragraphs are his
  text. Where a book ran on into the editor's introduction to the next work,
  or his note on the penitential discipline, it now ends at the last
  chapter.
  - Read on the page images: CCEL's slips put right ("coverts" for
    "converts", "can show" for "call show", "did he answer", "of the king's
    vineyards", "shalt not eat", "incorporeal", "an harlot", "undeservedly"
    for "unreservedly", "joining"). The page's own misprints are kept and
    named in the source lines: "rest" for "lest", "begining", "multipled",
    "condemed".
  - Greek words stay in CCEL's Latin letters, as the library's other volumes
    of the series have them, and the source lines say so; the 1896 page
    prints them in Greek.
- **St John of Damascus**, in Mary H. Allies's English (London: Thomas
  Baker, 1898): On Holy Images, his three apologies with the testimonies of
  the Fathers after each, and his Three Sermons on the Dormition (the 1898
  English calls them On the Assumption, and the source line says so). The
  text is Project Gutenberg's (ebook 49917; its transcriber notes minor
  emendations of spelling and punctuation, and the source line says that
  too). Its Greek is in Greek letters, as the page prints it.
- **The ingest learned five things**, each proved on November's works and
  December's Lives coming out byte for byte the same: an edition's chapter
  summary left out (`synopsis`), a book found after a marker when its
  heading repeats in the volume (`within`), a prologue before chapter I
  (`lead: true`), Gutenberg's letters written by their Unicode names and its
  italics in underscores, and a line-end "¬" in a scan's text read as the
  hyphen it is.
- **The reading room**: six books more. St Ambrose has no settled icon yet,
  so his four stand on the plate with his Lives; St John's wear his cover.
- **The apps**: about 1.1 MB of text more, 0.2 MB packed: the exports are 67.0
  MB packed of 85.
- **Walked** on the Android export: St Ambrose's page (Writings: the four)
  and each of the six works. checkMock finds only the reader's own faults,
  told before (the chapter buttons, the "Source:" line, the printers' em
  dashes).
- **Stills**: `december-saints/05-st-john-on-holy-images.png`,
  `06-st-ambrose-on-the-duties-of-the-clergy.png`.
