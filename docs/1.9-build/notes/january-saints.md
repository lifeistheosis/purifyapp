## The petition, in every language (push 2)

Found on push 1's walk: the closing petition at the foot of every saint's
page put the registry name whole after its own "Holy", so it read "Holy St.
Basil the Great, pray to God for us.", "Holy Holy Apostle Paul" and "Holy The
Prophet Zephaniah", in every language ("San St. Basil the Great"). The name now
goes in without its honorific (nameCore). And, on the arbiter's word of
2026-11-11, each language prays to a woman as a woman and to two or more
saints as more than one, the adjective and the verb agreeing, as its liturgy
says it; before, every catalog had only the masculine singular.

The petition is a liturgical formula, not a text of the Fathers: each form is
the language's own common form of "Holy N., pray to God for us" (Church
Slavonic "Святый ... моли Бога о нас", Greek "Άγιε ... πρέσβευε υπέρ ημών").
Whose the page is comes from the saint's `pronoun` (his, her, their) and, for
Arabic's dual, from PAIRS in lib/saints/pronoun.ts (the fifteen pages of
exactly two saints). The test, lib/saints/__tests__/intercession.test.ts,
walks every saint in all 21 languages and fails if a woman or two saints are
prayed to in the masculine singular where the language tells them apart.

Where I am unsure, said rather than guessed:
- **Indonesian, to two or more** (looked at again by Fable voices,
  2026-11-11, who has these files from here): now "Para kudus", where it was
  "Para Santo". An Indonesian text of the Litany of the Saints sets "Santo"
  or "Santa" before each name, which one line with the names in one place
  cannot do, and gives "Omnes Sancti et Sanctae Dei" as "Para kudus Allah":
  "para kudus" is its word for holy men and women together, where "Santo" is
  a man's title and read wrong over a husband and wife (Sts. Adrian and
  Natalia). The text read is an online encyclopedia's
  (p2k.stekom.ac.id/ensiklopedia/Litani_orang_kudus), not the bishops'
  printed book, so this is still not settled from print: a native reader
  should confirm it.
- **Portuguese** (the same look): left as it is, and the rule is now known.
  Brazilian litany texts read "São Pedro e São Paulo, rogai por nós" and
  "Santo André" (a script of the Diocese of Santo André; Portal Kairos has
  "rogai a Deus por nós"): "São" directly before a name that begins with a
  consonant, "Santo" before a vowel, and "Santo" before a title ("Santo
  Apóstolo"). The names are English in every catalog and many begin with a
  title (Apostle, Prophet), so one line cannot follow the name; "Santo" is
  right before a title and a vowel and wrong before a consonant-initial name
  ("Santo Basil the Great"). It can be mended only by choosing the word from
  the name, which is worth doing when the names themselves are in Portuguese.
  The same holds for Italian (San, Sant', Santo) and Spanish (San, but Santo
  Tomás and Santo Domingo), which had not been named here.
- **Urdu, to a woman** (the same look): now "مقدسہ", where it repeated the
  masculine "مقدس". Urdu's adjectives from Arabic take the feminine, and the
  Urdu Hail Mary addresses her as "مقدسہ مریم". That is from memory of the
  prayer: no text of it could be opened on the day, so it is said here
  rather than claimed as checked. The verb is the honorific and does not
  change.
- **Nepali, and Urdu to two or more**: the honorific imperative serves one
  and many alike, so those forms are one; a native reader may prefer a plural
  word for a company (Urdu has "مقدسین").
- **A company of women alone** would need a feminine plural (Santas,
  Sfintelor) that no catalog has: every page of more than one today has a man
  among them, and the test names the four in the Holy Women that are mixed, so
  a page of women alone fails until its form is written.
- The names themselves stay English in every language, as before; only the
  words around them change.

## The Prophet Micaiah: the last search (2026-11-11, the arbiter's 14:51)

He is the last saint on "search" in the app, and the gate lets only a
permission through, so the arbiter asked for any icon of him with a painter,
a monastery or a publisher to write to, modern or old. None was found:

- **Every icon offered for January 5 is the Micah of the Twelve.** The OCA's
  (filed under August 14), Pravoslavie.ru's (the Sretensky Monastery's site:
  his scroll is Micah 5:2, "И ты, Вифлееме"), and Legacy Icons' two, by Fr
  Theodore Koufos and by Brian Whirledge, each described as the minor prophet
  and listed on January 5 and August 14, the two being merged. The Greek
  calendar does not keep him on January 5 (imoph.org's Iconological
  Synaxarion), and searches in Russian ("Михей, сын Иемвлая", "Михей
  Старший", azbyka.ru) found no icon of him.
- **The old free pictures show the blow, his death, or the other Micah**: the
  medieval menologia (Vatican gr. 1613, p. 298; Walters 521, fol. 36r: an
  executioner leading him to an abyss); Weigel's plate of 1695; the 1582
  engraving after Maerten de Vos (MSK Gent, Public Domain Mark: "in maxillam
  percutitur", the fist raised, Micaiah from behind); Eskrich's woodcuts of
  1567 and 1573 and Scio's Bible of 1794 (Pitts Theology Library: Zedekiah
  "preparing to strike him"); Caspar Luyken's print of 1712 (Rijksmuseum:
  Zedekiah's hand raised over him); a 1690 Vulgate woodcut (234 px, Naboth's
  stoning beside him); the Nuremberg Chronicle's "Micheas" (Rijksmuseum, fol.
  53v, among the later prophets: the other Micah).
- **One without the blow**: Jan Luyken's etching for Weigel's Historiae
  Celebriores (1708; Rijksmuseum RP-P-OB-45.373, CC0 and the Public Domain
  Mark, 5528 by 6882), "De Propheet Micha, voor Achab, en Josaphat": Zedekiah
  stands beside him in his iron horns and does not strike, but Micaiah is
  seen from behind, his face turned to the kings. Put to the arbiter: a
  detail of him alone would be a figure from the back.
- **Otherwise** only a new icon, painted for us by a living iconographer, is
  left, which is the owner's to decide. Commons' search refused twice that
  day (429); January's push 2 had searched it and found only Weigel's plate.
- **Placed** (the arbiter's word of 15:04 UTC, "Luyken"): not his back
  alone, but the scene of his witness, cut from the Rijksmuseum's full scan
  (its IIIF image, 5528 by 6882): Micaiah before Ahab and Jehoshaphat, his
  arm held out to them, Zedekiah beside him in his horns, not striking. It is
  `prophet-micaiah.jpg`; its rights row credits the Rijksmuseum's CC0 and
  says what it shows and why there is no face. His book, Micaiah before
  Ahab, wears it, and he leaves both waiting lists: no saint in the app is
  left on "search".

## The twelve uncleared pictures (2026-11-11, the arbiter's 15:09)

`UNVERIFIED_ICONS` held 26 pictures shown with no source, licence or painter
recorded, several plainly contemporary icons. The arbiter gave this chat
twelve and Fable voices the other twelve (the prayer slideshow's two left

the file is shown to be an old public-domain work with its source, record it

plate with a "permission" row and an addressee. Never an uncleared picture
