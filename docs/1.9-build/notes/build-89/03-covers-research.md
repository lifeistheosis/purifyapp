How the rules were read here:

- **Licence:** public domain or CC0 as each file's own Commons page states it: the licence name and the page's licence categories, read through the Wikidata query service's bridge to the Commons API (Commons' own API answered this address with 429 all session). No file whose page names CC BY, CC BY-SA or GFDL is proposed; those are listed apart at the end, not yet allowed.
- **Size:** "600 x 750" asks whether a 4:5 cut at least 600 wide fits at the scan's own pixels inside the painted field (the shipped floor is 480 wide). "1024 square" asks whether a 1024 px square around the head fits at the scan's own pixels. Head sizes are estimates from the thumbnails.
- **Identity:** by the inscription on the picture where there is one, read in a zoom; otherwise by the scene, the museum's record or the page, and said so.
- **Repeats:** no file below is an existing cover's file. Where a saint who already has a cover appears again in a different picture, the row says so.

Which works are on the plate: `lib/reading/cards.ts` was run (read only) on the branch as it stands. 161 cards, 141 with a cover, 20 on the plate (below). Of the fifteen subjects 1.7.2 could not fill, St Nektarios of Aegina and St Paisios no longer have works in the room (`works: []`), so they need no cover.

Four of the plate subjects already have a verified picture in the app that the reading room does not use yet (`lib/saints/iconRights.ts`, settled 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-31): St Arsenius, St Cyril of Jerusalem, St Photius and St Justinian. Their rows below use those pictures.

## Main doubts for the owner

1. **Council pictures for people.** St Alexander, St Nectarius and St Celestine get their council's icon because no usable picture of them exists; St Irene gets the Seventh Council, where she is painted but tiny. The owner's own words allow "the historical context"; the header's rule speaks of a work's author first.
2. **The Ephesus painting is mislabelled on Commons** ("Council of Constantinople 381"). Its inscriptions name Theodosius the Younger, Pope Celestine and Cyril at Ephesus. The page gives no date for the painting.
3. **Small pictures.** St Cyril of Jerusalem and St Photius only at the app's bundled size; the five Ferapontov councils at 800 px; St Tarasius at 846 px if the whole halo must show (the 1713 px photographs cut its top). St Stephen's head is about 130 px in a whole figure.
4. **Repeated saints, new pictures.** Love (St John), Grief (the Theotokos), Despondency (St Antony) and Envy (the Forerunner) show saints who already have covers. Envy's own classic figure, Joseph the All-Comely, has no usable icon on Commons.
5. **Western pictures.** St Anianus's is Cima da Conegliano's painting of his healing (no halo, a turban), under the rule that allows a Western painting where no icon passed.
6. **Content to see before choosing.** Grief shows Christ's dead body; Envy's icon has the Forerunner's severed head on a dish (outside the cut); Fear's icon has the stokers below (outside the cut).
7. **Still on the plate:** St Symeon the New Theologian, St Irenaeus, St Hermione, St Isidora, St Olympias, St Papias, St Isaac the Syrian, and St John Cassian's Institutes and Conferences. For four of them a saint of the text already has a cover that could stand (St Philip for St Hermione, St Pachomius for St Isidora, St John Chrysostom for St Olympias, St John the Theologian for St Papias).

## How the search was done

The plate list came from the repo's own modules, run read-only with jiti (no cache written). Commons was searched through the Wikidata query service (MWAPI) at about one request every three seconds, in English, Russian, Greek, Serbian and Italian, by category (for example Icons of Joseph (son of Jacob), Icons of Elijah in the desert, Frescos of Saint Stephen, Ecumenical councils in frescos, the Peribleptos saint bishops), and by Commons "depicts" statements. Licences and categories were read from each file's own page. Thumbnails came from upload.wikimedia.org at its standard widths, one at a time, waiting out 429s. Downloads were only read as pixels (Pillow, `python3 -I`).
